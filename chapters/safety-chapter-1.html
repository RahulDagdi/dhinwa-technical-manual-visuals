  1. Foundational governance, roles, communication lines, code implementation, checklist discipline, and personal safety ownership.
  2. 1.1 Role Awareness: All personnel must clearly understand their assigned safety duties and site requirements before starting work.
  3. 1.2 Defined Responsibilities & Communication: Areas of responsibility are formally defined, and lines of emergency and operational communication are clear across all teams.
  4. 1.3 Proactive Accountability: Every team member takes personal ownership of safety and acts immediately when they spot or foresee a hazard.
  5. 1.4 Code Implementation: Personnel must be familiar with applicable construction codes of practice and implement them consistently on site.
  6. 1.5 Checklist Discipline: Review internal safety procedures and checklists prior to activity execution and use them without fail.
  7. 1.6 Scaffold Integrity Standard: Never permit the use of any scaffold or scaffolding practice on site that you would not feel safe using yourself.
  8. 1.7 Safety Culture: Think safety before every task and work safely at all times.
  1. Worker readiness, safety education, emergency escape procedures, mock drills, emergency contacts, and hazard signage.
  2. 2.1 Regular Safety Education: Educate workers continuously on safe work practices, task-specific hazards, and preventive controls.
  3. 2.2 Emergency Evacuation Plans: Emergency evacuation procedures, assembly points, and escape routes must be clearly defined and communicated to everyone on site.
  4. 2.3 Monthly Mock Drills: Conduct mock emergency drills once a month to test and streamline fire fighting, minor first-aid response, and hospital transfer for serious injuries.
  5. 2.4 Alertness and Near Miss Identification: Maintain a steady, controlled pace of work and train workers to stay continuously alert to changing site conditions.
  6. 2.5 Emergency Contact Display: Prominently display the names and phone numbers of key emergency personnel, safety officers, first-aiders, and external emergency services.
  7. 2.6 Safety Signage: Install clear, visible safety warning signs across the site to alert workers to potential hazards.
  8. 2.7 Water Source Protection: Cover or securely barricade all water storage tanks, sumps, and open water bodies to prevent accidental drowning.
  1. Core PPE standards (head, hearing, eye/face, respiratory, fall arrest, safety nets, and water safety).
  2. 3.1 PPE Baseline: Achieving a zero-accident site requires strict compliance with hazard-specific protective equipment alongside engineering controls.
  3. 3.2 Scope of PPE: Personal protective equipment covers head, eye, face, hearing, respiratory, hand, and foot protection, as well as protective clothing and fall-arrest gear.
  4. 3.3 Head Protection: Protective helmets (hard hats) with chin straps must be worn in all areas with risk of head impact, falling objects, or electrical contact.
  5. 3.4 Hearing Protection: Wear earplugs or earmuffs whenever workplace noise levels cannot be reduced below safe limits by engineering controls.
  6. 3.5 Eye and Face Protection: Use safety goggles or face shields during hacking, welding, glass cutting, chipping, grinding, or drilling; workers wearing prescription glasses must wear over-spectacle safety goggles.
  7. 3.6 Respiratory Protection: Maintain a written respiratory protection plan; select appropriate respirators, train users, and clean, disinfect, inspect, and store units properly.
  8. 3.7 Safety Belts and Lifelines: Workers at height must remain securely anchored to a rigid structure at all times; immediately remove from service any safety belt, lanyard, or lifeline that has experienced an impact/arrest load.
  9. 3.8 Safety Nets: Install certified safety nets where work surfaces exceed 25 feet (7.5 m) above ground and ladders, scaffolds, or guardrails are impractical.
  1. High-risk height controls, age eligibility, fall protection triggers, slab openings, scaffolding checks, and dowel protection.
  2. 4.1 Minimum Age Requirement: Workers below 18 years of age are strictly prohibited from working on the construction site.
  3. 4.2 Fall Protection at 6 Feet (1.8 m) or More: Whenever workers are exposed to a vertical drop of 6 feet or more, install one of three controls before work begins: rigid guardrails, safety nets, or personal fall arrest systems anchored above head/shoulder level.
  4. 4.3 Slab Openings & High-Rise Debris Nets: Barricade or securely cover all slab openings at every floor level; install safety nets on high-rise buildings, clear fallen debris every 2 days, and tighten net tension every 14 days (fortnightly).
  5. 4.4 Restricted Vertical Alignment Work: Prohibit simultaneous work in vertical alignment near slab edges, stairwells, lift shafts, or floor openings without close supervision and overhead protection.
  6. 4.5 Scaffolding Planks: Tie working platform planks securely to the scaffold with ropes; wooden planks must be at least 2 inches thick and inspected for splits or defects.
  7. 4.6 Scaffold Foundation & Bracing Checks: Erect scaffolding on firm, level ground and inspect twice a week for weak zones and intact horizontal bracing—especially checking that temporary supports through toilet ventilators are not removed during tiling.
  8. 4.7 Exposed Dowel Rod Protection: Never leave vertical or horizontal rebar/dowel rods exposed at body level; cap them or wrap them securely with thick hessian cloth and Plaster of Paris (POP).
  1. Excavation depth thresholds, engineering design requirements, sloping, shoring, and shielding.
  2. 5.1 Trench Depth Thresholds: Trenches 5 feet (1.5 m) deep or greater require a protective system; trenches 20 feet (6 m) deep or greater require a protective system designed by a registered professional engineer.
  3. 5.2 Selection of Protective Systems: Choose sloping, shoring, or shielding based on soil type, trench depth, water table, and adjacent loads.
  4. 5.3 Sloping: Protect workers against cave-ins by cutting back the trench walls at a safe angle inclined away from the excavation.
  5. 5.4 Shoring: Prevent soil movement by installing engineered timber, aluminum, or hydraulic structural supports against the trench walls.
  6. 5.5 Shielding: Protect workers inside the excavation by placing trench boxes or steel shields capable of withstanding soil cave-ins.
  1. Day-to-day operational rules covering trench inspection, gas testing, ladders, electricals, lifting, chemicals, first aid, and site hazard controls.
  2. 6.1 Daily & Post-Rain Trench Inspections: Inspect trenches at the start of every shift, after any rainstorm, and following any vibration or hazardous event.
  3. 6.2 Pre-Entry Atmospheric Testing: Test deep trenches and confined excavations for low oxygen, flammable vapors, and toxic gases before any worker enters.
  4. 6.3 Spoil Pile & Equipment Setback: Keep excavated soil (spoils), heavy machinery, and materials at least 2 feet (0.6 m) away from the trench edge.
  5. 6.4 Safe Trench Access (4 Feet / 1.2 m or Deeper): Provide stairways, ladders, or ramps in all trenches 4 feet deep or deeper so workers never climb soil walls.
  6. 6.5 Ladder Safety: Use sturdy, defect-free ladders kept clean of oil and grease, positioned on firm footing, and tied securely at the top.
  7. 6.6 Electrical Safety: Use only industrial safety plugs and sockets, and route power through insulated, safely supported electrical conduits.
  8. 6.7 Lifting Machinery & Gear Testing: Periodically test and certify lifting machinery (cranes, hoists) and lifting accessories (wire ropes, slings, shackles) before use.
  9. 6.8 Access Equipment Rectification: Regularly inspect scaffolds, ladders, and walkways, and immediately tag out and rectify any damaged components.
  10. 6.9 Artificial Illumination: Provide adequate artificial lighting wherever natural daylight is insufficient for safe work or movement.
  11. 6.10 Fire Fighting Readiness: Place appropriate fire extinguishers at hazardous zones, check expiry/pressure gauges regularly, and ensure personnel know how to operate them.
  12. 6.11 Carpentry Yard Waste Removal: Clear wooden shavings, sawdust, and offcuts from the carpentry yard daily to eliminate fire and slip hazards.
  13. 6.12 Chemical Handling PPE: Wear chemical-resistant gloves, face masks/respirators, and eye protection whenever mixing, pouring, or applying site chemicals.
  14. 6.13 Vector, Pest & Snake Control: Implement regular pest-control measures against mosquitoes, flies, rodents, snakes, termites, and bees across the site and labor camp.
  15. 6.14 First Aid & Snake-Bite Kits: Maintain fully stocked first-aid kits and snake-bite emergency kits on site, and train multiple staff members in their proper use.
  16. 6.15 High-Risk Work Assignment Controls: Avoid assigning workers without specialized height-safety training and full fall-arrest systems to hazardous locations such as steep or sloping roofs.
  1. Physical perimeter and hazard separation for trenches, staircases, lift shafts, and work zones.
  2. 7.1 Trench Barricading: Install rigid barricades at least 1 meter back from the trench edge and at a minimum height of 1 meter.
  3. 7.2 Staircase Barricading: Barricade all open staircase flights and landings with sturdy handrails until permanent railings are installed.
  4. 7.3 Lift Shaft Barricading: Barricade all lift shaft openings immediately after deshuttering using full-height rigid barriers and warning signage.
  5. 7.4 Hard vs. Soft Barricading: Use solid rigid barricading for fall hazards, deep excavations, and lift shafts, and use caution tape only for low-risk perimeter demarcation.
  1. Site-wide illumination, staircases, cellars, material storage, and hazardous/fuel area lighting rules.
  2. 8.1 Site-Wide Floodlighting: Illuminate the entire active construction site adequately with weatherproof floodlights during low-light or night operations.
  3. 8.2 Priority Staircase Lighting: Treat lighting across all staircases, ramps, and vertical access routes as top priority to prevent trips and falls.
  4. 8.3 Cellar & Basement Lighting: Maintain continuous, reliable lighting inside all basements, cellars, and enclosed underground structures.
  5. 8.4 Material Storage & POL Zone Lighting: Light all material storage yards clearly, while using only certified flameproof/explosion-proof fixtures (never open halogen lamps) near diesel, petrol, and POL storage areas.
  1. Placement across high-risk zones, manuals, supplier training, and inspection/AMC tracking.
  2. 9.1 Mandatory Fire Extinguisher Locations: Install appropriate fire extinguishers at the site office, diesel/petrol (POL) storage areas, carpentry/timber yard, and paint/chemical storage areas.
  3. 9.2 On-Site Operating Instructions: Keep clear operating instruction manuals and pictorial usage charts posted alongside extinguishers on site.
  4. 9.3 Emergency Fire Response & Team Training: Ensure the Project Manager and at least 5 site supervisory staff receive hands-on fire-extinguisher training from qualified suppliers.
  5. 9.4 Expiry Tracking & AMC: Include monthly pressure/expiry inspections and refilling schedules as a mandatory part of the Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC).
  1. Material stacking, debris yard management, housekeeping manpower ratios, and site conduct rules.
  2. 10.1 Common-Area Dustbins: Place clearly labeled dustbins in all common walkways, rest areas, and work zones.
  3. 10.2 Continuous Debris Clearance: Clear accumulated construction debris regularly so work areas and walkways remain clean at all times.
  4. 10.3 Dustbin Capacity Ratio: Provide waste bins at a minimum capacity of 150 liters for every 5,000 sq. ft. of built-up area.
  5. 10.4 Central Debris Yard & Daily Carting: Collect waste at a central debris yard accessible to tractor-trailers and haul debris off site daily.
  6. 10.5 Neat Material Stacking: Stack materials neatly in designated bays away from vehicle traffic routes and pedestrian walkways.
  7. 10.6 Dedicated Housekeeping Manpower: Supervise a dedicated housekeeping squad daily—allocating 1 worker per 30,000 sq. ft. during structural construction and 1 worker per 20,000 sq. ft. after structure completion.
  8. 10.7 Trade-Wise Preliminary Cleaning: Require each trade contractor to complete preliminary work-area cleanup before the housekeeping squad performs general cleaning and debris carting.
  9. 10.8 Granite Slab Stacking: Stack granite and stone slabs vertically on level ground using engineered Mild Steel (MS) A-frame stands balanced evenly on both sides.
  10. 10.9 Lockable Temporary Apartment Doors: Install temporary lockable doors at apartment entrances during finishing stages to control unauthorized entry and limit cleaning liability.
  11. 10.10 Contained Sand Stacking in Rooms: Store masonry/plastering sand in a corner of the room enclosed by a perimeter of blocks to prevent sand from spreading across floors.
  12. 10.11 Zero-Tolerance Tobacco & Smoking Ban: Smoking and chewing tobacco are strictly prohibited across the entire construction site.
  13. 10.12 Deterrent Penalties for Violations: Enforce strict penalties against littering, creating site nuisances, or violating safety rules.
  1. Clear access roads, stacking height limits, debris clearance, and visible warning boards.
  2. 11.1 Unobstructed Access Roads: Keep all site and office access roads completely free of parked vehicles, stacked materials, and debris at all times.
  3. 11.2 Maximum Material Stacking Height (1.5 m): Limit the stacking height of all general construction materials to a maximum of 1.5 meters to prevent toppling.
  4. 11.3 Cement Bag Stacking Limit (10 Bags): Stack cement bags neatly on dry pallets to a maximum height of 10 bags.
  5. 11.4 Clean H-Frame Stacking: Stack all scaffolding H-frames uniformly in designated racks so components do not protrude into walkways.
  6. 11.5 Scheduled Debris Yard Clearance: Empty and grade the debris yard at regular intervals to prevent overflow and pest harborage.
  7. 11.6 Mandatory Site Warning Boards: Display bold, high-contrast safety boards at vantage points reading: STOP, WEARING OF HELMETS MANDATORY ON SITE, DO NOT ENTER WITHOUT A HELMET, STAY AWAY, and HOIST AT WORK.
  1. Frontline site compliance checkpoints (helmets, warning boards, safety belts, goggles/earplugs, gloves, gum boots, and first-aid/stretcher availability).
  2. 12.1 Mandatory Site-Wide Hard Hats: Enforce 100% safety helmet compliance across every zone of the construction site without exception.
  3. 12.2 Vantage-Point PPE Signage: Place helmet and PPE reminder boards at all site entry gates, staircases, and hoist landings.
  4. 12.3 Compulsory Safety Belts at Height: Verify that every worker operating at height wears a full-body safety harness/belt securely anchored.
  5. 12.4 Grinding & Shearing PPE: Require safety goggles and earplugs for all personnel operating shearing, cutting, or grinding machines.
  6. 12.5 Task-Specific Hand Gloves: Ensure workers wear appropriate protective gloves for all hazardous, sharp, hot, or abrasive tasks.
  7. 12.6 Gum Boots for Concreting: Mandate waterproof, slip-resistant gum boots for all personnel engaged in concrete pouring and screeding.
  8. 12.7 Site Stretcher & First-Aid Station: Maintain a rigid emergency stretcher and fully stocked first-aid kit at a clearly marked location on every site.
  1. Final operational verification covering woodworking/grinding machine guards, fire protection, internal checklist discipline, and management review checkpoints.
  2. 13.1 Grinding Machine Guarding & PPE: Verify that all grinding machines have intact wheel guards and that operators use full eye, face, and hearing protection.
  3. 13.2 Electric Saw-Cum-Planer Hazard Control: Enforce strict guarding on combination electric saw-and-planing machines, where operating one blade often leaves the opposite cutting blade dangerously exposed.
  4. 13.3 Mandatory Cutting-Machine Safeguards: Ensure a sturdy wooden or metal cover is fitted over the saw blade at all times, a protective cover is fitted over the planing device, all blades are cleaned and covered when idle, and a dedicated fire extinguisher is stationed beside the machine.
  5. 13.4 Management Checklist Governance: Review internal safety checklists regularly with the Project Manager—recognizing that checklists protect worker safety, save execution time, and prevent costly rework and delays.
